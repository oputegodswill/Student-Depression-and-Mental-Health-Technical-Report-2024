Introduction:

Depression among students across various age groups is steadily increasing. This analysis seeks to identify the key causes of student depression and propose possible solutions to address the issue. The dataset was sourced from Kaggle, and the analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel, with the aid of pivot tables and charts.

Story of the Data: 

A publicly available dataset from the Kaggle.com repository was used, structured in rows and columns. The dataset includes variables such as Gender, Academic pressure, Work Pressure, CGPA, Study satisfaction, Job satisfaction, Sleep duration, dietary habits ,work/study hours, family history of mental illness,  depression,  financial stress,   among others. A key limitation of this project is the small size of the dataset, along with the absence of date-related information, which restricts a comprehensive analysis of trends over time.

Post- Analysis Insights/Recommendation
1. Impact of Family History on Depression
The analysis shows that students with a family history of mental illness (8,273 cases) have slightly higher depression cases compared to those without family history (8,063 cases). However, the difference between the two groups is relatively small, indicating that genetic or hereditary factors alone do not fully explain depression among students.
Across both groups, the 18–27 age range accounts for the highest number of depression cases (5,588 cases with family history and 5,389 without), while cases significantly decline in older age groups. This suggests that younger individuals are the most vulnerable population to depression regardless of family background. It is recommended that mental health support should not only target students with known family history but extend preventive programs to the entire student population, particularly those in the 18–27 age group. Early screening programs, counseling services, and awareness initiatives should be implemented in universities to detect and manage depression at an early stage.
2. Gender-Based Depression Patterns
Male students account for a higher proportion of depression cases compared to female students across multiple indicators. For example:
Male students recorded higher cases associated with unhealthy dietary habits (7,297 cases).
Male students also showed higher depression levels under academic pressure, particularly at pressure level 5 (2,947 cases).
Additionally, male students showed slightly higher representation in both family history and non-family history categories.
This indicates that male students may be experiencing higher stress levels or may have fewer coping mechanisms for managing academic, financial, and lifestyle pressures.
Educational institutions should therefore implement gender-sensitive mental health interventions. Programs encouraging emotional expression, peer support groups, and mental health awareness campaigns targeting male students may help reduce stigma and encourage help-seeking behavior.
3. Influence of Dietary Habits on Depression
The analysis clearly indicates a strong relationship between dietary habits and depression:
Unhealthy diet: 7,297 cases
Moderate diet: 5,558 cases
Healthy diet: 3,473 cases
Students with unhealthy dietary habits consistently show higher depression counts across academic pressure levels, particularly levels 3 and 5.
This suggests that lifestyle factors, particularly nutrition, may significantly influence mental health outcomes. On this note, Universities should promote healthier lifestyle habits among students through:
Nutrition awareness programs
Access to healthier food options on campus
Wellness campaigns promoting balanced diets and physical activity
Encouraging healthier eating habits may contribute to improved mental well-being and reduced depression rates.
4. Academic Pressure and Student Depression
Academic pressure appears to be one of the strongest contributors to student depression. The highest number of cases occurs at academic pressure level 5, followed by levels 3 and 4. This indicates that higher academic demands significantly increase psychological distress among students.
The analysis also reveals that students experiencing high academic pressure combined with unhealthy dietary habits exhibit the highest depression counts, highlighting the interaction between academic stress and lifestyle factors. It is recommended
Educational institutions should adopt academic stress reduction strategies, including:
Flexible academic scheduling
Academic counseling and mentorship programs
Time-management and stress-management training
Balanced coursework and assessment structures
These interventions can help reduce excessive academic pressure and improve student well-being.
5. Financial Stress and Depression
Financial stress also shows a strong association with depression, with the highest cases recorded at financial stress level 5 (2,745 cases) followed by level 4 (1,894 cases). This indicates that increasing financial burden significantly increases the likelihood of depression among students.
Universities and policy makers should strengthen financial support systems, including:
Student scholarships and grants
Emergency financial assistance
Financial literacy training
Part-time job opportunities for students
Reducing financial pressure can significantly improve mental health stability among students.
6. Geographic Distribution of Depression
The analysis indicates that certain cities report higher concentrations of depression cases, with notable numbers observed among male students in Kolkata, while female cases appear relatively prominent in Vasai-Virar and Srinagar. This suggests that geographic location and local socio-economic conditions may influence student mental health trends.
 Therefore,
City-specific interventions should be developed, including:
Expanding access to local counseling services
Strengthening mental health awareness programs in high-risk areas
Collaborating with educational institutions to provide targeted mental health support

     Conclusion
   
The post-analysis reveals that student depression is influenced by multiple interconnected factors, including: Academic pressure, Financial stress, Dietary lifestyle, Gender differences, Age group vulnerability, Family history of mental illness
However, academic pressure, unhealthy lifestyle habits, and financial stress appear to be the most significant drivers of depression in this dataset.
A holistic student mental health strategy should be implemented that integrates:
Academic stress management,Financial assistance programs, Lifestyle and wellness education, Accessible counseling services, Early mental health screening programs. Such comprehensive strategies will help reduce depression levels and promote healthier academic environments
